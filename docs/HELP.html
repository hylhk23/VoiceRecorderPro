Voice Recorder Pro 1.1.1

A voice-activated sound recorder for Windows 11: it records only while someone is speaking, writes straight to disk, and lets you play, mark and cut recordings without another editor.

Getting started

  1. Press Record (the red button, or R). With Vox on, the recorder waits for sound (title shows [VOX]) and records each time you speak ([REC]).
  2. Press Stop (S). The file is saved in Documents\VRP Recordings with a name like VOX20261123-001.mp3 (VOX, the date, and a number that starts again at 001 each day).
  3. Press Play (Space) to listen. Drag the Position slider to move around.

Closing the window keeps the app running in the notification area (tray). Use File > Exit to quit. Recording continues when the window is hidden or the screen is locked, and Windows will not go to sleep while recording.

Recording phone calls, meetings or conversations may require everyone's consent where you live.

Voice activation (Vox)

When the input rises above the threshold, a new segment starts. It includes a short pre-roll (audio from just before the sound) and continues for the hang time after the last sound, so short pauses inside speech do not split a segment. Bursts shorter than the minimum segment (clicks, bumps) are thrown away.

SettingDefaultEffect
Threshold4Higher = only louder sound triggers recording. Use Options > Vox > Calibrate to measure your room.
Attack time20 msHow long sound must last before recording starts.
Hang time2.0 sHow much silence ends a segment.
Pre-roll300 msAudio kept from before the trigger, so first syllables are not cut.
Minimum segment0.5 sShorter sound bursts are dropped.
Each activation startsA new segmentOr a new file for every activation.

Options > Disable Vox always records continuously; Hide Vox removes the Vox controls from the window.

Files, names and segments

Recordings are named from a pattern (default VOX{yyyy}{mm}{dd}-{n3}, for example VOX20261123-001.mp3; the number starts again at 001 each day). Tokens: {yyyy} {yy} {mm} {Mon} {Month} {dd} {HH} {MM} {SS} {n} {n3} {host} {device} {tag}. A / creates subfolders, e.g. {yyyy}/{mm}/{Mon}{dd}_{n}. Existing files are never overwritten.

Pressing Record with a file open appends a new segment to it (when the format matches); change this in Options > Recording. Segment boundaries and time stamps are stored inside the file: MP3 chapters (ID3 CHAP), WAV cue points with labels, or FLAC CHAPTER tags, so other programs can see them too.

Automatic new files: by size, after a duration, at midnight or every N hours, with no audio lost between files. While recording, data reaches the disk every second; after a crash or power cut the app offers to recover the file at the next start.

Separate channels: microphone left, computer audio right

Normally the microphone and computer audio are mixed into one track. Choose Options > Format > Channels > Separate: microphone left, computer audio right to record a stereo file with your voice alone on the left channel and the computer audio (the other people in a call) alone on the right. Transcription software can then transcribe each side on its own and tell you apart from the others.

  • Use a headset. With speakers, the call is also picked up by your microphone and appears in both channels.
  • Mic and Computer still work as before. An unticked source records silence on its channel; the file stays stereo.
  • Voice activation starts when either side is speaking. The level meters show each side; the number shows the louder one.
  • For MP3, the bitrate is raised to 64 kbit/s when you choose Separate, because the two channels share it. You can change it; below 64 kbit/s speech loses clarity. FLAC keeps both channels exactly.
  • The file says which channel is which (in its tags), so other programs can read it. The Sample field shows L/R for such a file.
  • Recording appends to an open file only if it uses the same channel setting. Join refuses to combine separate-channel files with mixed ones. Saving a copy as mono mixes the two sides together.
  • Playback plays your voice in the left ear and the other side in the right.

Clean-up: noise suppression and automatic level

For an office with background noise, or a speaker some distance from the microphone, open Options > Format > Clean-up (microphone):

  • Noise suppression removes steady background noise such as air conditioning, fans and computer hum (it uses RNNoise). It helps much less with other people talking nearby, and nothing can bring back words the microphone did not pick up.
  • Automatic level brings near and far voices to a similar loudness. It raises a quiet voice by up to 24 dB, lowers a voice that is very close, holds its setting during pauses so background noise is not pumped up, and never lets peaks go above −1 dBFS.
  • Also keep an untouched copy (on by default) writes a second file next to the recording, named like VOX20261123-001-original.mp3, with the sound exactly as it came in. The two files line up sample for sample, so you can compare them or go back to the original.

Clean-up applies to the microphone only, from the next recording. Vox listens after noise suppression, so steady noise no longer starts a recording; it listens before the automatic level, so the threshold keeps its meaning. Noise suppression delays the recording by about 0.2 s; computer audio is delayed with it, so the two stay in step. The file's tags say what clean-up was applied.

Best results still come from the microphone: put it close to the people speaking, turn Vox off for meetings, and use a higher MP3 bitrate (64 kbit/s) or WAV/FLAC.

Live transcript (new in 1.1)

Voice Recorder Pro can show the words as they are spoken, in English, Cantonese and Mandarin, also mixed in one sentence, and save them as text beside the recording. Speech is recognised on your PC by the free Live Transcript pack; nothing is sent over the internet, and no graphics card is needed.

Set it up

  1. Download the Live Transcript pack zip for this version from the same Releases page as the program (about 180 MB) and unblock it (right-click > Properties > Unblock).
  2. Close Voice Recorder Pro, then extract the pack into the folder that holds the VoiceRecorderPro folder, so it merges into it (choose Replace if asked). It adds a transcribe folder and a few libraries inside python.
  3. Start the program and choose Transcript > Live Transcript On. Options > Transcript shows whether the pack is installed; Check pack files compares every file with its checksum.

The window

With the live transcript on, the switch at the top right (Both | Recorder | Transcript, or View menu, Ctrl+1 / Ctrl+2 / Ctrl+3) chooses what you see:

  • Both: the recorder on the left and the transcript on the right.
  • Recorder: the recorder alone, with one line of the newest words at the bottom.
  • Transcript: the transcript alone, with a small recording bar at the bottom (state, time, Mic and Computer levels, Record/Pause and Stop).

The window remembers its size for each choice. Words still being recognised are shown in italics and end with “…”; they are corrected when the speaker pauses. The line above the text says what is being saved. View > Caption Bar opens a small window that stays on top of other programs, for following a Teams or Zoom call; A− and A+ change the text size.

With Options > Format > Channels > Separate, the transcript labels the microphone Me (a filled blue tag) and the computer audio Others (orange bold text). With one mixed track there are no labels.

What is saved

Choose in Options > Transcript > What to save:

ChoiceFiles
Audio and textThe recording (for example VOX20261128-001.mp3), its transcript VOX20261128-001.txt and subtitles VOX20261128-001.srt.
Text, with audio kept for checking (default)The same files, but the audio is moved to the Recycle Bin automatically after the number of days you set (30 by default, up to 365), or when you choose Transcript > Mark Checked and Delete Audio. The transcript stays. Empty the Recycle Bin when you want the audio gone for good.
Text onlyOnly the .txt. No sound is saved at all, so Vox segments, playback and editing do not apply. A new transcript never replaces an older one with the same name: it gets -2, -3 and so on.

The .txt is written phrase by phrase while you record, with the time of each phrase, so a power cut loses at most the last phrase. The panel follows the newest line, with an empty line below it; scroll up to read earlier text, and it stays where you are until you scroll back to the bottom. The .srt subtitles are timed to the audio file, so a media player shows them in step with the recording. Only files the program registered itself are ever moved to the Recycle Bin automatically. On a network drive Windows has no Recycle Bin, so files there are deleted at once, as with File > Delete File.

Privacy

  • Off the record: pause the recording (R, the Record button, or Ctrl+Alt+R from any program). Nothing is recorded or transcribed until you resume; the transcript shows “Off the record … nothing saved” with the times.
  • Bookmark (B in the window, Ctrl+Alt+K from any program) marks a moment in the transcript, for example an action item.
  • Mask numbers replaces phone numbers, Hong Kong ID numbers, card numbers and e-mail addresses with placeholders in the transcript.
  • Windows encryption (EFS) makes new recordings and transcripts readable only by your Windows account. It needs Windows 11 Pro, Enterprise or Education and an NTFS drive. Back up your encryption certificate, or the files cannot be opened after Windows is reinstalled.
  • Delete transcripts after a number of days, if your organization keeps notes only for a limited time. They go to the Recycle Bin.

Better results

  • Names and terms (Options > Transcript > Edit names) corrects names the recogniser gets wrong, for example people, companies and products.
  • Choose Chinese characters: Traditional (Hong Kong), Traditional (Taiwan) or Simplified. Cantonese is written in colloquial Cantonese characters such as 係, 喺 and 嘅.
  • Leave the language on Automatic for mixed English and Cantonese; choose one language only if the automatic choice keeps getting it wrong.
  • For a final transcript, open the recording in Secure Offline Transcriber (Transcript > Open in Secure Offline Transcriber): it can transcribe again with a larger model, and has proofreading and export to Word.
  • Speech recognition makes mistakes, especially with background noise, people talking at once and unusual names. Check important passages against the audio before you rely on them.

The speech engine runs as a separate program at below-normal priority, so it never holds up the recording. On a slow PC the text may fall behind; the panel then says how many seconds, and the words-while-spoken preview pauses until it catches up.

Playback and transcription

  • Auto-backspace: every time playback resumes after a pause it steps back 2 s (Options > Playback).
  • Speed: 50–200% with the pitch kept (Control > Playback Speed).
  • PgUp/PgDn jump between segments; Home/End to the start/end.
  • With a selection, Play plays only the selection; Edit > Loop Selection repeats it.

WAV and MP3 edits copy the original data directly (no re-encoding), so they are very fast and lose no quality. MP3 marks snap to MP3 frames (about 36 ms at 16 kHz). Edits never change the original until they have finished successfully.

Foot pedals and external devices

Options > External Devices replaces the old PC game port:

  • Game controllers (USB gamepads, joysticks, button boxes): any button, axis or hat.
  • USB foot pedals (HID): a built-in profile covers the VEC Infinity pedal; for others, select a mapping row, click Learn and press the pedal.
  • Serial ports: CTS, DSR, DCD and RI as inputs (e.g. a radio's squelch output with Record while active); DTR/RTS as outputs that follow Recording, Armed or Playing (e.g. an "on air" lamp). A switch between DTR and an input line registers as active.

Pedals that type keys can be bound in Hot-keys instead. Typical transcription setup: left = Rewind (hold), middle = Play while held, right = Fast Forward (hold).

Timed and scheduled recording

Control > Timed Recording records for a set time from now. Control > Schedule starts and stops recordings by itself (once, daily, weekdays, weekly or monthly), each with its own Vox, device, format and file names. Schedules run while the app is open or in the tray; if the PC was asleep, a recording starts late when no more than 5 minutes have passed.

Recordings can also stop by themselves after a period of silence or at a clock time (Options > Recording).

Command line

Use vrp.exe (next to VoiceRecorderPro.exe) from a Command Prompt, a script or a desktop shortcut:

vrp record --duration 0:30:00 --format wav     start recording (starts the app if needed)
vrp pause | vrp resume | vrp stop
vrp status --json
vrp play "C:\Recordings\Oct02_1.mp3" --from 1:20
vrp extract Oct02_1.mp3 --from 1:00 --to 2:30 --out clip.mp3
vrp split Oct02_1.mp3 --at 5:00
vrp join a.mp3 b.mp3 --out joined.mp3
vrp convert Oct02_1.wav --format flac
vrp devices
vrp schedule add --name Morning --start 2026-10-21T09:00 --duration 0:30:00 --repeat weekdays
vrp record --headless                           record without any window; Ctrl+C saves and stops
vrp record --layout split                       microphone left, computer audio right (--layout mix: mixed)

Exit codes: 0 ok, 1 usage error, 2 device error, 3 file error, 4 not running, 5 busy. Options > Recording > Run after each file is finished runs your own command with {path}, {duration_s}, {segments} and {start_iso}.
